# Aeromedical Factors and Human Performance

**Episode:** 20
**Version:** 0.1.5

## In this episode

- Use a personal preflight to examine illness, medication, stress, alcohol, fatigue, and emotion before committing to a flight.
- Connect altitude, congestion, overbreathing, and carbon monoxide exposure to the cues and decisions they can change.
- Distinguish the four forms of hypoxia and plan a route with several suitable alternates for a changing pilot or flight condition.
- Reassess the pilot in flight and end the flight at the nearest suitable airport when a mechanical exposure could impair the pilot.
- Study spatial and visual illusions as limits on sensation and visual evidence.

## Study materials and visual aids

- [Private Pilot ACS Human Factors task](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/acs/private_airplane_acs_6.pdf#page=16) — PA.I.H, printed p. 8. Use the knowledge, risk, and skill elements to study cause, symptom, effect, and corrective action together.
- [AIM fitness for flight and personal checklist](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-1) — AIM 8-1-1. The illness, medication, stress, alcohol, fatigue, and emotion prompts used before departure.
- [FAA OTC medication questions for pilots](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/medical_certification/media/OTCMedicationsforPilots.pdf#page=1) — Questions 1-2 on PDF p. 1 separate the underlying condition from the proposed medication.
- [FAA medication guidance and reference materials](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/medical_certification/medications#content) — The FAA directory for medication guidance, the OTC reference guide, and the reference table.
- [14 CFR 61.53 medical deficiency for operations requiring a medical certificate](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.53) — Section 61.53(a)(1)-(2). Read the operation-specific condition and treatment language.
- [14 CFR 91.17 alcohol and drugs](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-91/subpart-A/section-91.17) — Section 91.17(a)(1)-(4). Each listed crewmember prohibition is separate.
- [AIM altitude, ear and sinus, and scuba guidance](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-2) — AIM 8-1-2. Review the separate subparagraphs for hypoxia, ear block, sinus block, and decompression sickness after scuba diving.
- [PHAK hypoxia types](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/19_phak_ch17.pdf#page=3) — Chapter 17, printed p. 17-3. Learn hypoxic, hypemic, and stagnant hypoxia by cause.
- [PHAK histotoxic hypoxia](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/19_phak_ch17.pdf#page=4) — Chapter 17, printed p. 17-4. Complete the four-form vocabulary.
- [AIM ear and sinus block details](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-2) — AIM 8-1-2, subparagraphs 2-3. Congestion can obstruct pressure equalization during descent.
- [AIM flight after scuba diving](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-2) — AIM 8-1-2, subparagraph 4. The recommended interval depends on dive type and flight altitude.
- [AIM hyperventilation in flight](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-3) — AIM 8-1-3. Note the overlap with early hypoxia symptoms.
- [AIM carbon monoxide poisoning in flight](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-4) — AIM 8-1-4. The heater-related cues and immediate action are central to the scenario.
- [AIM illusions in flight](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-5) — AIM 8-1-5. Includes spatial illusions and illusions leading to landing errors.
- [AIM vision in flight](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html#8-1-6) — AIM 8-1-6. Illumination and scanning affect the visual information available to the pilot.
- [PHAK dehydration and heatstroke](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/19_phak_ch17.pdf#page=14) — Chapter 17, printed p. 17-14. Review the symptoms and prevention discussion.
