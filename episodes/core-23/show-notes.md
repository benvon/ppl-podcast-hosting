# Emergencies and Abnormal Situations

**Episode:** 23
**Version:** 0.1.2

## In this episode

- Follow an engine problem from the first abnormal indication to an early precautionary diversion.
- If power is lost, compare available altitude, wind, terrain, and obstacles with an attainable landing area.
- Make a useful urgency or distress call, use the installed emergency equipment, and keep the airplane's approved checklist in the decision.
- Compare an electrical failure and a smoke case as separate examples of how the priority and time available can change.

The spoken lesson uses a fictional daytime VFR flight in a single-engine trainer. The airplane's own current AFM or POH gives the speeds, limitations, configuration, and checklist sequence for an actual flight.

## Study materials and visual aids

- The [Airplane Flying Handbook guidance on gliding](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/04_afh_ch3.pdf#page=23) explains why best glide and minimum sink answer different questions.
- The [Airplane Flying Handbook emergency landing discussion](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/19_afh_ch18.pdf#page=4) covers terrain, obstacles, configuration, and control near touchdown.
