# Private-Pilot Privileges, Limitations, and Currency

**Episode:** 21
**Version:** 0.1.3

## In this episode

- Follow Elena as she checks whether she can take a friend on a daytime VFR trip with a possible late return.
- Match the certificate, medical basis, flight review, and aircraft qualification to the specific flight.
- Check the two passenger takeoff-and-landing recency rules against logbook evidence.
- Apply the private-pilot expense-sharing limit to a proposed rental payment.
- Separate legal currency from proficiency in the airplane and avionics Elena would actually use.

## Study materials and visual aids

- [Private Pilot ACS Pilot Qualifications task](https://www.faa.gov/training_testing/testing/acs/private_airplane_acs_6.pdf#page=10) — The knowledge, risk, and skill elements for this lesson.
- [14 CFR 61.3 pilot certificate and photo ID](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.3) — Check subsection (a)(1)-(2) for the U.S. civil-aircraft document rule.
- [14 CFR 61.31 category, class, and type ratings](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.31) — Read subsections (c)-(d) for the rating and PIC questions.
- [14 CFR 61.31 high-performance qualification](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.31) — Read subsection (f) for the engine threshold, training, endorsement, and stated alternatives.
- [14 CFR 61.23 private-pilot medical requirement](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.23) — Read subsection (a)(3) for the private-pilot medical basis.
- [FAA third-class medical certificate validity](https://www.faa.gov/ame_guide/app_process/general/validity#content) — Item 3 explains the remainder-of-month rule and the 24- or 60-calendar-month duration based on age at examination.
- [14 CFR 61.56 flight review](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.56) — Read subsections (a) and (c)-(e) for review content, timing, and alternatives.
- [14 CFR 61.57 general passenger recency](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.57) — Read subsection (a)(1)-(2) for takeoffs, landings, aircraft match, controls, and the tailwheel full-stop condition.
- [14 CFR 61.57 night passenger recency](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.57) — Read subsection (b)(1) for the exact time period and full-stop condition.
- [14 CFR 61.51 pilot logbook records](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.51) — Read subsections (a)-(b) for documenting recent experience.
- [14 CFR 61.113 private-pilot compensation limit](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-E/section-61.113) — Read subsection (a) with its stated exceptions before analyzing a particular flight.
- [14 CFR 61.113 pro-rata expense sharing](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-E/section-61.113) — Read subsection (c) for the pilot's share and eligible expense categories.
- [14 CFR 61.23 BasicMed qualification](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-A/section-61.23) — Read subsection (c)(3) for the driver's-license, listed medical eligibility, course, and examination conditions.
- [14 CFR 61.113 BasicMed operating limits](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-61/subpart-E/section-61.113) — Subsection (i) limits the aircraft to no more than seven authorized occupants and 12,500 pounds maximum takeoff weight, and the flight to no more than six passengers, no more than 18,000 feet MSL, no more than 250 knots indicated airspeed, and operations within the United States unless the other country authorizes them. Check the required logbook records there as well.
- [FAA BasicMed study overview](https://www.faa.gov/licenses_certificates/airmen_certification/basic_med#content) — Follow the FAA's checklist, examination, course, and limitations links.
